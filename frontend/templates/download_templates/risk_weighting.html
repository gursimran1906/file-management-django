How the practice management system scores a client and matter risk assessment, and why each answer is weighted as it is. LSAG 5.13 expects a practice to be able to explain its weightings and suggests short notes on the rationale for each; these are those notes. This document is generated from the scoring itself, so it cannot describe weights other than the ones actually applied.

How a rating is reached

Each answer that indicates risk carries a weight in proportion to what it tells you. A section's contribution is capped so no single run of answers can carry the rating, and a section that is serious on its own sets the rating on its own at the points shown with it, whatever the total comes to. A total under {{ bands.medium_at }} is low, {{ bands.medium_at }} to {{ high_band_max }} medium, and {{ bands.high_at }} or more high. Client risk and matter risk are scored separately and the overall rating is the higher of the two.

Some answers do not wait for a total. Where one of the following is answered as shown, the matter is high risk outright and enhanced due diligence applies; the form will not accept a lower level.

Keeping this current

Review this alongside the firm-wide risk assessment at least annually, and whenever the SRA updates its sectoral risk assessment. Changing any weight means changing the version and its date, so that an assessment scored under an earlier version can still be explained by the weighting that produced it. Assessments completed before {{ version_date|date:"j F Y" }} were rated by hand and are marked as such.
